01 — Design system

Built from what was already here

Two pieces of real brand material sat unused in the repo: dive.png, a diving-mask icon with a genuine blue-to-violet-to-pink gradient, and a dark-navy theme color already committed in the PWA manifest. Nobody had built a system around them. This one samples the gradient directly from the icon's pixels — the values below aren't chosen, they're measured.

#0B1220
App background. Matches the PWA manifest's theme_color exactly.

The Dive Gradient — blue #3B73F2 → violet #8365BE → pink #DC577F. Sampled pixel-by-pixel from dive.png, not invented. Used once per screen at most, only where a moment of brand weight is earned — a hero, a single primary call-to-action — never as a general UI color. A gradient-everywhere interface would contradict the product's own low-noise principle.

Display — headlines only

Find hyper-local shore-dive sites.

Space Grotesk · 600 · used with restraint

Body — everything else

This timer alerts you, in this app, if you miss your check-in. It does not notify anyone else, and it's less reliable if you close the app or lock your phone.

Geist Sans · already wired in the real app · zero migration cost

Dark is correct, not moody

Used outdoors, at the water's edge, often in bright sun. A dark, high-contrast UI reads better than a washed-out light one — function, not just mood.

44px minimum, 56px for safety

Wet or gloved hands, one-handed use. Safe-Return controls get the largest touch targets in the app — not a place to save vertical space.

Never a guarantee it can't back

No "help is coming," no "you'll be notified." Say plainly what's true — this device only, best-effort, weaker on iPhone.

Calm, not gamified

No streaks, no urgency-hacking. Checking in on the Safe-Return timer is a relief, not a win — no confetti, ever.

Flow 01

Onboarding & sign-in

Google-only sign-in, and permissions requested one at a time, at the moment each feature is first used — never a three-in-a-row gauntlet at launch. Every "not now" leaves the app fully functional.

Key decision: location, notifications, and microphone are each primed contextually — opening the map, starting the Safe-Return timer, tapping "record voice log" — because a permission prompt shown with no immediate reason to grant it gets reflexively denied, and browsers rarely let a site re-ask.

Flow 02

Map exploration & site detail

Every pin carries lineage before a tap — ring color for provenance, fill for hazard signal, a corner glyph only for the four restricted legal-access states. Nothing renders as a bare color pin.

Key decision: pin fill color encodes a hazard-report signal only (calm vs. "something's been reported") — never a red/green safety verdict, since community hazard reports carry no severity field and aren't independently verified. Implying certainty the data can't back was ruled out from the start.

Flow 03 — life-safety-critical

Safe-Return timer

The hardest tone call in the product: an expired timer has to read as urgent without ever implying help is coming, because in v1 none is. Every disclosure string here was preserved verbatim from the real, built components — not softened for a cleaner screen.

Key decision: on the alarm screen, urgency comes from color and a slow ~1.2s breathing pulse — not new copy, not a fast strobe. "Silence alarm" and "check in — I'm safe" get deliberately different visual weight (zinc vs. emerald) so the two are never confused: one quiets noise, the other is the only action that actually resolves the emergency.
